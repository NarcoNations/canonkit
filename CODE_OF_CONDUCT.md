# CanonKit Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

We are committed to making participation in CanonKit respectful, constructive, and free from harassment for everyone, regardless of experience, identity, background, ability, or personal characteristics.

## Expected behaviour

Participants should:

- communicate with empathy, clarity, and respect
- give actionable feedback about ideas and work rather than attacking people
- welcome questions and different levels of experience
- acknowledge mistakes and make reasonable efforts to repair harm
- protect private, sensitive, customer-specific, and proprietary information
- respect the project's clean-room boundary and security policy

## Unacceptable behaviour

The following behaviour is not acceptable:

- harassment, intimidation, discrimination, or personal attacks
- sexualised language or unwanted attention
- deliberate disruption, trolling, or sustained bad-faith engagement
- publishing another person's private information without permission
- posting credentials, vulnerabilities, private source material, or exploit details publicly
- retaliation against someone who raises a concern in good faith

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in repository issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, community spaces, and public interactions where someone is representing CanonKit.

## Reporting and enforcement

For security vulnerabilities, use the private process in `SECURITY.md`.

For other conduct concerns, contact the repository maintainers privately through an available GitHub reporting channel. Do not open a public issue containing sensitive personal information.

Maintainers may edit or remove contributions, comments, commits, issues, or other participation that violates this Code of Conduct. They may also issue a warning, impose a temporary restriction, or permanently remove a participant, depending on the severity and pattern of behaviour.

Reports will be handled as confidentially as reasonably possible. Enforcement decisions should prioritise community safety, proportionality, consistency, and a clear record of the behaviour involved.
